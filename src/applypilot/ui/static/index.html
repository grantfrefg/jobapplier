    How it works: what each step does
      
    Each step picks up what the step before it left, so you can run them all or tick just some. Jobs you've
        already processed aren't done again.
    
      
    
        
    1. Discover (no AI)
    
        
    Searches LinkedIn, Indeed Australia and Jora with the job titles and places from Setup → Job search.
          Jobs outside your locations, or with an excluded title, are dropped. The same role listed on several sites
          is kept once (LinkedIn first, then Indeed, then Jora). SEEK isn't searched: its terms forbid it.
    
        
    2. Enrich (AI only when needed)
    
        
    Opens each job's page to get the full description and the link to apply. Most pages are read without
          AI; the AI is asked only for pages it can't read otherwise.
    
        
    3. Score (1 request per 10 jobs)
    
        
    Rates each job 1–10 against your resume and profile. Deal-breakers (citizenship or a security clearance
          you don't have, the wrong city when you won't move, far too senior or junior) score 3 or lower.
          Titles on your exclude list get 0 without asking the AI.
    
        
    4. Tailor (about 2 requests per job)
    
        
    With Let me choose which jobs get tailored ticked, this step waits for you: Run stops after
          scoring, and only the jobs you tick in Choose jobs to tailor are tailored, whatever their score.
          Otherwise it runs for every job scoring at least the Minimum score. Either way it writes a resume
          reworded and reordered for the ad,
          plus a cover letter. It may change order, emphasis and wording, never facts. Code then checks every title,
          employer, date, skill and number against your real resume, and an AI fact-check reads the result. A draft
          that fails is written again (up to 4 drafts in all); if none passes, nothing is used (see "What the log means").
    
        
    5. Cover (only for older jobs)
    
        
    Cover letters now come with step 4. This step only writes letters for jobs tailored before that.
    
        
    6. Pdf (no AI)
    
        
    Turns both documents into A4 PDFs laid out for applicant tracking systems (plain, one column, standard
          headings). Each resume also gets an ATS score, shown on the Apply tab.
    
        
    Then: Apply tab
    
        
    Jobs with documents appear there. Apply opens a browser, fills the form from your profile and
          saved answers (the AI only for questions it hasn't seen) and uploads your PDFs. You always review and
          press Submit yourself.
    
        
    Searching again
    
        
    A job board that blocks the app or errors is skipped for the rest of that run, and the searches it
          missed are remembered. Run a step again → Discover: retry the searches that failed runs just those,
          on just those boards (a note under Run says how many there are). Discover: search every board again
          repeats every search, the same as ticking only Discover and pressing Run; jobs already found are skipped
          either way.
    
        
    Running a step again
    
        
    Each step skips jobs it has already done. Run a step again for jobs already done (under Run)
          repeats one step for every job not applied to yet; steps after Score only take jobs at or above the
          Minimum score. For a single job, use Run again… on its row on the Apply tab. What a job has is kept
          until the new version is ready: a failed re-score keeps the old score, and a cover letter that fails its
          checks keeps the old letter. Fact-check again is the AI review from step 4 on the documents the job
          has now (1 request); a job it flags keeps its documents and says what was found, so you can Redo it or
          apply as it is.
    
        
    Strictness
    
        
    normal: the usual checks. strict: overused words such as "leverage" also count as errors.
          lenient: skips the AI fact-check (about half the requests); the code checks still run.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    What the log means
      
    
        
    "9 results -> 7 new, 0 dupes, 2 filtered (location)"
    
        
    One search on a job board. New jobs were added; dupes were already saved; filtered ones
          were outside your locations or excluded titles.
    
        
    "LinkedIn: blocked" or "429 (blocked: use a proxy)"
    
        
    The job board is limiting how often it can be searched. It's skipped for the rest of the run and
          the other boards carry on. It usually lifts within a few hours; then use Run a step again →
          Discover: retry the searches that failed to search just what was missed.
    
        
    "5/20 [APPROVED] attempts=1 requests=2"
    
        
    Job 5 of 20 got a tailored resume and cover letter. Attempts is how many drafts were written;
          requests is how many AI requests that took (each draft is written, then fact-checked).
    
        
    [FAILED_JUDGE]
    
        
    The fact-check compared every draft with your real resume and found something invented or stretched
          (a duty, a skill, a more senior title). Those drafts are never used. Common when a job asks for more than
          your resume shows. The line after it says what was wrong.
    
        
    [FAILED_VALIDATION]
    
        
    Every draft broke a fixed rule (for example a missing section, or a skill or employer that isn't in your
          resume).
    
        
    [ERROR]
    
        
    A request failed (network, or the AI's reply couldn't be read).
    
        
    After a failure
    
        
    That job gets no documents and isn't offered on the Apply tab. It's tried again on the next run, up to
          5 times. Apply → All jobs shows the reason; Try again there retries it now. The rejected
          draft is kept as …_REJECTED.txt in the tailored_resumes folder.
    
        
    Getting more jobs tailored
    
        
    A few rejections are normal: they're jobs that ask for more than your resume shows. If many jobs
          fail, in order of what to try first:
    
          1. Make your resume and profile complete. The checks only allow what's in them. Add real
          experience, tools and achievements you left out of the resume (Setup → Resume), and list every skill
          you really use in the profile's skills, then tick My skills list is accurate. Then press
          Try again on the job (Apply → All jobs).
    
          2. Press Try again. Each try writes new drafts, and a borderline job often passes the second
          time.
    
          3. Set Strictness to lenient (above) before pressing Run or Try again. It skips the
          AI fact-check, the step that rejects "stretched" claims. The code checks still remove invented titles,
          employers, skills, tools and numbers, but a reworded duty that overstates what you did can get through,
          so read those resumes before you send them. It also halves the requests per job.
    
          4. Skip jobs you can't honestly match. If you're not senior, add "Senior" (or "Lead",
          "Principal") to Skip jobs whose title contains in Setup → Job search, so those jobs aren't
          tailored at all, or
          raise the Minimum score so only close matches are tailored.
    
          A job that failed 5 times isn't tried again automatically; Try again still works on it.
    
        
    "Daily Gemini budget … used up"
    
        
    Today's free requests are used (some are kept for applying). It resets at midnight US Pacific time
          (late afternoon or evening in Australia); the remaining jobs wait for the next run.

      How to use this tab
      
      
        
      The list
      
        
      Score is how well the job fits you (1–10). Click the job title to open the ad. Resume and
          Cover letter open the PDFs written for that job; ATS is the resume check (below). The views:
          Ready to apply (has documents, not applied yet), Ready: no employer account (quick ones),
          Applied, and All jobs (including ones that got no documents, with the reason).
      
        
      Applying with the app
      
        
      1. Optional: press Check where to apply to see which jobs need an employer account (no AI).
      
          2. Press Apply on a job. A browser opens the ad, follows its Apply link, fills in your details and
          saved answers, uploads the resume and cover letter, and moves through Next pages. The AI is asked only
          about questions it hasn't seen before.
      
          3. When it stops, the panel at the top says what's left (a field it couldn't answer, a sign-in, a button
          only you can press). Do it in the browser, then Fill this page again if a new page appeared.
      
          4. Check everything in the browser and press the site's Submit yourself. The app never submits.
      
          5. Back here, press I submitted it so the job moves to Applied. Not yet: save for
          later keeps it ready; Never apply to this job removes it from the list.
      
        
      Accounts and logins
      
        
      Workday, PageUp, iCIMS and similar sites need an account with each employer: the app types your email,
          never a password. Sign in or create the account in the browser, then Fill this page again. For
          Google sign-in, use Log in on the job (or Setup → Log in to sites) first.
      
        
      Applying yourself instead
      
        
      You don't have to use the Apply button. Click the job title to open the ad in your usual browser,
          download the Resume and Cover letter from this list (or copy text from them), and apply and
          submit everything yourself. Then press Mark applied so the job moves to Applied and isn't
          offered again. The same PDFs for the job you're applying to are also in the ApplyPilot uploads
          folder in your home folder.
      
        
      Using your own resume or cover letter
      
        
      Under a job's documents, use my own resume or use my own cover letter lets you pick a PDF
          or Word file you wrote yourself. Apply then uploads yours instead of the generated one (you can
          mix, e.g. your resume with the generated letter). A job with your own resume isn't sent to Gemini for
          tailoring, and its ATS score checks your file. It also works for a job whose drafts were rejected: give
          it your resume and it's ready to apply. use generated switches back. Your files are kept in the
          own_documents folder inside .applypilot.
      
        
      Run again…
      
        
      Repeats one step for that job: get the ad again, score again, write just the cover letter again,
          fact-check the documents again, make the PDFs again, or redo the ATS check. The confirmation says how many
          AI requests it uses. To rewrite the resume and letter together, use Redo.
      
        
      Edit and Reads human
      
        
      edit resume / edit cover letter under a job's documents opens the text: change it in your own
          words and the PDF is made again. The Reads human badge flags what may make the documents sound
          AI-written; hover it for details. See Guide → Sounding like you, not an AI.
      
        
      Redo and Try again
      
        
      Redo rewrites a job's resume and cover letter (for example after you update your resume or
          profile); the old ones stay until the new ones pass the checks. Redo all documents does it for
          every job you haven't applied to. Each costs about 2 AI requests per job.
      
      
      
    
      
    
      What the ATS score means
      
      Most employers put applications through an applicant tracking system (ATS): Workday, SuccessFactors,
        PageUp, JobAdder, Greenhouse and others. It reads your resume into fields, and recruiters then search and
        filter on keywords, location and work rights. The ATS badge (out of 100) estimates how well this resume
        survives that. It uses no AI and isn't any one vendor's ranking: it's a guide.
      
      
      
        
      What's checked
      
        
      Keywords (most of the score): of the tools and must-haves in the ad that you really have, how many
          appear in the resume in the ad's own words. Another form of the same thing ("AWS" when the ad says
          "Amazon Web Services") counts half: some systems match it, but a recruiter's search for the ad's word
          may not, so hover tells you which word to use.
      
          The PDF itself: its text is read back the way a parser reads it, to check your name comes first,
          the email and phone can be read, the sections come out in order and each job keeps its dates. Odd
          characters a parser may misread (such as the "ﬁ" ligature) are flagged too.
      
          Readability: standard headings (Summary, Skills, Experience, Education), email and phone at the
          top, a readable date range for every job, 1–3 pages.
      
          Filters Australian employers use: work rights (e.g. "Australian Citizen") and city/state shown; no
          date of birth or photo.
      
          Australian spelling.
      
        
      Reading it
      
        
      ATS 80+ is good. Lower usually means keywords you have are missing, or
          something like work rights isn't on the resume. Hover the badge to see exactly what to look at.
      
        
      Skills you don't have
      
        
      Hover also lists what the ad asks for that isn't in your resume. These don't lower the score and are
          never added: putting them in would be untrue. If you do have one, add it to your resume or your profile's
          skills list, then Redo.

      ApplyPilot runs on your computer and does the slow parts of applying for jobs:
      
    
        
      
      1. Finds jobs on LinkedIn, Indeed Australia and Jora that match the titles and places you choose.
        2. 
      
      2. Scores each job 1–10 against your resume, so you see the best matches first.
        3.

        4.

        Anthropic (Claude) work too with your own paid key. Press Save, then Test key.
        2. 
      
      2. How to apply. Leave Browser engine: you submit selected unless you want a paid tool to apply
        and submit for you (Claude Code or Codex; they ask you to confirm first).
        3. 
      
      3. Browser. Press Test browser. If no browser is found, press Install browser (about
        150 MB, once). Then press Log in to sites and sign in to Google, LinkedIn and SEEK in the window that
        opens, and close it. The app stays logged in afterwards.
        4. 
      
      4. Your resume. Paste your full resume as plain text (copy it out of Word or your PDF) and press
        Save resume. The more complete it is, the better: the app only ever uses what's in it.
        5. 
      
      5. What jobs to look for. Your city (e.g. Sydney, NSW), the job titles you want, and words to
        skip (e.g. Senior, Lead, Intern). Press Save search.
        6.

      Your profile, and why it's asked
      
    
      Each part of the profile does a specific job. Under every section in Setup a short note says what it's
      for; in more detail:
      
    
      
      
      SectionWhat it's used for
      
      
        
      PersonalTyped into application forms. Your name, city and state, email, phone and
          LinkedIn / GitHub head every resume; your preferred name signs cover letters. The street address is only
          typed into forms that ask for it. With the browser engine the AI sees placeholders such as
          {{EMAIL}}, never the real values. The password is only for the Claude Code and Codex
          engines (to sign in to or create employer accounts); leave it blank with the browser engine.
      
        
      Work authorisationThe first thing Australian employers screen on. It answers the
          work-rights questions on forms, appears on your resume ("Australian Citizen", "Permanent Resident"), and
          makes jobs you can't take score 3 or lower: citizens-only roles if you're not a citizen, clearances you
          don't hold, sponsorship you need but isn't offered. If you need sponsorship, cover letters say so in one
          plain sentence rather than leaving it to a surprise later.
      
        
      AvailabilityAnswers notice-period, start-date, relocation and driver's licence
          questions. Full time and contract feed the score (a contract-only role scores 4 or lower if
          you don't want contracts). Willing to relocate decides whether jobs in other cities count. Your local
          area is your whole city and the places you search in (Setup → What jobs to look for), so a suburb like
          Wentworthville is local to a job in the Sydney CBD. A cover letter mentions relocating when the job is
          elsewhere.
      
        
      CompensationSee Salary.
      
        
      ExperienceYears of experience and target role tell the scorer your level, so
          jobs far too senior or junior score low. Current job title is the only title a tailored resume's
          headline may use (never the job's title, which would be a claim you haven't earned). Education
          level goes on the resume's education line and answers education questions. Current company
          answers "current employer" questions.
      
        
      Skills boundaryThe skills and tools a tailored resume or cover letter may name. See
          Skills below: it's the part worth the most care.
      
        
      Resume factsChecks on every tailored resume. Employers and the university listed
          must appear (a draft that drops one is rejected and written again); projects and real numbers listed can
          be quoted. Only put things that are in your resume; clear the examples if you're unsure.
      
        
      EEO voluntaryOptional diversity questions some forms ask (gender, Aboriginal or
          Torres Strait Islander, disability). "Prefer not to say" is always fine. They're only used to answer
          those questions, never for scoring or documents.
      
            
    
      

    
      Skills: the key to better tailored resumes
      
    
      When ApplyPilot tailors a resume it rewrites and reorders it for the ad, but it may only name a skill or tool
      that's in your resume or in your profile's skills list. Anything else is removed by the code
      checks, and drafts that lean on it are rejected. So the skills list decides how well a resume can match:
      
    
        
      
      • Add skills your resume leaves out. Resumes are short, so plenty of real skills never make it in.
        If you've used Terraform, Tableau or Kafka at work, list it: the next resume for an ad that asks for it can
        put it in the skills section, in the ad's own words. That's what applicant tracking systems search for,
        so the ATS score goes up too.
        • 
      
      • Use the names employers use. "Amazon Web Services (AWS)", "Power BI", "CI/CD". Short and long
        forms of common terms are matched anyway, but the ad's exact word counts most.
        • 
      
      • Only list what you could talk about in an interview. The list is the boundary of what you claim.
        A skill here can appear on any resume.
        • 
      
      • Remove the template's examples (JavaScript, Docker, AWS, CI/CD, MongoDB, Git, Linux…) unless they're
        really yours, then tick My skills list is accurate. Until it's ticked, those examples are ignored so
        they can't end up on a resume by accident.
        • 
      
      • After changing it, use Redo on jobs you haven't applied to (or Redo all documents) to
        get resumes that use the new list. Hovering the ATS badge shows skills an ad wanted that you didn't list:
        if you do have one, add it here.
        • 
    
      
    
      The categories (languages, frameworks, tools…) are only for your own tidiness: every skill counts the same,
      and engineers in other disciplines can put CAD tools, PLCs or standards in any of them.
      

    
      Salary
      
    
      The Compensation part of your profile, all base salary in AUD, excluding super:
      
    
        
      
      • Salary range min: your minimum, the lowest base you'd accept. It's the one number every part of
        the app treats as your floor.
        • 
      
      • Salary expectation: what to ask for when an ad doesn't say what it pays.
        • 
      
      • Salary range max: the top of the range, for forms that ask for one.
        • 
    
      
    
      They're used in two places only:
      
    
        
      
      1. Scoring. An ad that clearly pays less than your minimum scores 5 at most, so it isn't picked by
        default. Pay is compared like with like: a package "including super" is about base × 1.12, and a day rate
        about the annual salary ÷ 230. Most ads don't state pay; then pay is ignored.
        2. 
      
      2. Salary questions on forms. Your expected salary is given, never less than your minimum; a range
        question gets min–max. If the form asks for a package or "including super", 12% super is added (e.g.
        $120,000 base becomes $134,400). Free text says e.g. "$120,000 plus super". The Claude Code and Codex
        engines also answer with the midpoint when the ad shows a range above your minimum, and work out
        hourly and day rates for contracts.
        3. 
    
      
    
      Salary never appears on a resume or cover letter. The minimum must be no more than the expected salary and the
      maximum; Setup won't save figures that contradict each other.

      Employer accounts: Workday, PageUp, iCIMS and similar sites need an account with each employer. The app
      types your email but never a password: sign up or sign in in the browser, then Fill this page again.
      For "Sign in with Google", use Log in on the job first.
      
    
      Saved answers: answers you type or change in the browser before submitting are remembered and reused
      next time, ahead of the AI's. They're listed (and can be deleted) at the bottom of Setup.
      

    
      Applying yourself instead
      
    
      You never have to use the Apply button. Click the job title to open the ad in your usual browser, download the
      Resume and Cover letter from the list, apply your own way, then press Mark applied so the
      job moves to Applied.
      
    
      To send your own resume or letter for a job, use use my own resume / use my own cover
      letter under its documents (PDF or Word). Apply then uploads yours, and its ATS check reads your file.
      use generated switches back.
      

    
      Redo, Try again and Run again
      
    
        
      
      • Redo (on a job) rewrites its resume and cover letter, e.g. after you improve your resume or profile.
        The old ones stay until the new ones pass the checks. Redo all documents does every job not yet
        applied to.
        • 
      
      • Try again appears on a job whose drafts were rejected; it writes new ones now.
        • 
      
      • Run again… (menu on a job) repeats one step for that job: get the ad again, score again, write just
        the cover letter again, fact-check the documents again, remake the PDFs, or redo the ATS check.
        • 
      
      • Run a step again for jobs already done (on Find jobs, under Run) does the same for every job not
        applied to yet. Discover: retry the searches that failed repeats only the searches a job board
        blocked or failed last time.
        • 
    
      
    
      Every one of these asks first and says how many AI requests it will use.
      

    
      Getting more jobs tailored
      
    
      A job gets no documents when every draft failed the fact checks: usually the job asks for more than your
      resume shows. Apply → All jobs shows the reason when you hover its status. In order of what helps most:
      
    
        
      
      1. Make your resume and profile complete: add real experience, tools and achievements you left out, list every
        skill you really use, and tick My skills list is accurate. Then Try again.
        2. 
      
      2. Press Try again: a borderline job often passes the second time.
        3. 
      
      3. Set Strictness (Find jobs) to lenient. It skips the AI fact-check; the code checks still
        remove invented titles, employers, skills and numbers. Read those resumes before you send them.
        4. 
      
      4. Skip jobs you can't honestly match: add words like Senior to Skip jobs whose title contains,
        or raise the Minimum score.
        5. 
      
      5. Or give the job your own resume: it's then ready to apply straight away.
        6. 
    
      

    
      Sounding like you, not an AI
      
    
      Recruiters read a lot of generated applications, and what puts them off is text that's generic, samey or
      obviously copied from the ad. Four things help:
      
    
        
      
      • Tailoring style (Find jobs, next to Strictness). Full rewrite rewords bullets and the summary
        for each ad: the best keyword match. Light touch keeps your bullets word for word, only swapping in the
        ad's term for the same thing and reordering, and changes at most one sentence of your summary. Resumes then
        read like you wrote them, because you did. A light-touch draft that rewrote too much is sent back.
        • 
      
      • Your writing (Setup). Paste a cover letter or a few paragraphs you wrote, and cover letters follow
        your tone, sentence length and way of opening and closing. Its facts are never used. Each new letter is also
        checked against your recent ones, so they don't all open the same way.
        • 
      
      • Reads human (the badge next to ATS on the Apply tab). A check with no AI for the patterns that make
        text read as generated: stock phrases ("proven track record"), "not just X but Y", long dashes, the same
        verb starting bullet after bullet, every bullet the same length, strings of "X, Y and Z", most letter
        sentences starting with "I", long phrases copied from the ad, and a letter opening like another recent one.
        Hover it to see what to change, and how much of the resume is in your own words.
        • 
      
      • Edit (edit resume / edit cover letter under a job's documents). Change any line in your
        own words and the PDF is made again, with the ATS and Reads human checks. One sentence that only you would
        write often does more than any setting. Your edits aren't fact-checked, so keep them true.
        • 
    
      
    
      Settings apply to documents written from then on; use Redo for ones you already have.
      

    
      The ATS score
      
    
      Most employers put applications through an applicant tracking system (Workday, SuccessFactors, PageUp,
      JobAdder…) that reads your resume and lets recruiters search it. The ATS badge (out of 100) estimates how
      well this resume survives that: whether the ad's keywords you really have are there in the ad's own words,
      whether the PDF reads back cleanly, standard headings, work rights and location shown. 80+ is good. Hover the
      badge for what to fix. Skills the ad wants that you don't have are listed but never added.

      • Everything is kept on this computer. What leaves it: the job-board searches, the AI requests, and the
        applications you send.
        • 
      
      • With the browser engine, your name, email, phone, address and ID numbers go to the AI only as placeholders;
        the real values are typed in on your computer.
        • 
      
      • The browser engine never types or sends a password: you sign in yourself, and logins stay in the app's
        own browser profile. The profile's password field is only used by the Claude Code and Codex engines;
        leave it blank otherwise.
        • 
      
      • Resumes and letters are checked against your real resume: titles, employers, dates, skills and numbers you
        don't have are removed or the draft is rejected.

      When something goes wrong
      
    
      
      
      No jobs found
      
      
      Check the city and job titles in Setup → What jobs to look for. Very specific titles find little; add a
        broader one. Jobs outside your location are filtered out (see the log's "filtered" counts).
      
      
      "LinkedIn: blocked" (or 429 / 403) in the log
      
      
      The job board is limiting searches. The other boards carry on. Wait a few hours, then use Run a step
        again → Discover: retry the searches that failed.
      
      
      A job shows "No documents"
      
      
      Its drafts failed the fact checks. See Getting more jobs tailored.
      
      
      "Ready, but the fact-check flagged it"
      
      
      A fact-check run again found a problem. Hover the status to read it, then Redo the job or apply with
        the documents as they are.
      
      
      The browser doesn't open, or opens and closes
      
      
      Setup → Browser → Test browser; if that fails, Install browser. Close the Log in to
        sites window first: they share one browser profile.
      
      
      "Daily Gemini budget used up"
      
      
      Today's free requests are gone. The remaining jobs wait until it resets, or switch to a paid key.
      
      
      An application filled something wrong
      
      
      Fix it in the browser before submitting: your answer is saved and used next time. Delete a wrong saved
        answer at the bottom of Setup.
      
      
      Something else
      
      
      The log on Find jobs has the details. Most steps can be repeated safely with Run again.
